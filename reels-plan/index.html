Organic reel ads: two references, four ads

29 Oct 2026. Teardown of the two reels Farooq sent, which parts need an AI presenter, screen recordings or motion graphics, how we improve the hooks, and four ready scripts (two for Ecom Agent OS, two for the Winning Statics bundle). Nothing has been generated or spent yet.

The short version. Reel A (AgentOS, an AI digital avatar over product screens) gets the proof right: a face on screen the whole time and the real product doing the explaining. Reel B (EdBytz, faceless kinetic text over screenshot cards) gets the feed right: 9:16, the whole promise in the first frame, framed as a tip someone discovered rather than a pitch. We build one format from each, for each product, and we replace both reels' fast salesy voices with a calm professor who actually explains. Every ad gets three swappable hooks.

Worth taking

  • A face stays on screen the whole time, while the real product does the explaining in the main frame.
  • Every feature is a before and after. The viewer gets the pain first, then the fix.
  • The FAQ cards answer objections in the viewer's own words.
  • Its shared-memory story is our Brand Brain story almost word for word.

What fails

  • The hook names the product before giving anyone a reason to care, and "game changer" is the most scrolled-past phrase on the feed.
  • 7 minutes at 16:9: the UI can't be read on a phone, and most viewers never reach the point.
  • The AI audio glitches. One looping sentence is enough to lose a viewer's trust.
  • The "Digital Avatar" label and a banner that stays on screen the whole time both say "this is an ad".
  • Hype ("window won't stay open") where an explanation should be.

Its visual grammar is fixed in every frame, so it is easy to rebuild as a template:

  • A show tag at top left ("edbytz ai tools"), and a section chip at top right that changes with each beat.
  • A two-line headline: white, with one accent word in yellow.
  • One real screenshot card in browser chrome, with a yellow highlight box on exactly what the voice is talking about.
  • Word-by-word captions near the bottom, and a huge faint italic ghost word (the beat's keyword) behind them.
  • One motion device per beat: a count-up, a sticker, a checklist, a step badge or a stamp.

Why it feels organic: it's framed as a discovery ("someone put..."), it reads as a tip and not a pitch, the name only arrives as the answer to a question, there's no logo, and the ending asks for a comment instead of a purchase. What we'd change: the narration is fast and clipped. It gets the facts across, but it never explains why they matter.

4. What we keep, what we change

  • From A: a presenter who stays on screen (full frame for the human moments, a circle while the product is shown), real product screens as proof, a before and after for each feature, and objections answered in the viewer's own words.
  • From B: native 9:16, the whole promise in the first frame, discovery framing, screenshot cards with highlight boxes, section chips, count-ups, captions and the ghost word.
  • Voice: a professor explaining something to one student: full sentences, natural breaths, a little slower than conversation (about 140 to 150 words a minute). It explains why each thing matters, not just what it is. No clipped fragments and no hype words.
  • Length: 50 to 75 s per ad, so the voice has room to explain, plus a 30 to 45 s cutdown of each.
  • Dropped: the permanent ad banner, "game changer", urgency hype, abstract 3D filler, the "digital avatar" label, and any take with an audio glitch (every take goes through two transcribers before it's used).

5. The hook system

Every ad gets three hooks. Only the first 4 to 6 seconds change and everything after is shared, so three hooks cost little more than one ad. Each hook fires three layers in the first second:

  1. On-screen headline: 2 to 7 words, the biggest thing in the frame, readable with the sound off.
  2. Spoken line: the professor opens a question the viewer wants closed. It's said calmly. What makes it catchy is what it says, not how fast it's said.
  3. One visual interrupt: a count-up, a sticker, a side-by-side, or a finished ad that un-builds itself.

We rotate four hook types: Discovery ("Someone has packaged..."), Puzzle ("Have you ever noticed why..."), Proof first (frame 0 is the finished result, then "this began as one photo") and Call-out ("If you run a small store...").

7. How each piece gets made

PieceHow
AVATARSeedance 2.5 face through the cinematic ads harness, with the speech generated inside the video so it sits on the lips. Each presenter gets an identity card and keyframes, and the voice from the first approved take is passed into every later take. Units: one short unit per hook (5 to 8 s, so it's cheap to re-roll and the brand name sits inside it), plus two body takes of about 25 to 30 s. The circle and the full-frame shots are cut from the same takes, so lip sync never breaks. Brand words are prompted phonetically ("EE-kom AY-jent oh-ess") and checked on the isolated voice track by two transcribers.
SCREENEcom Agent OS: six verified captures of the real library already exist (agents, Brand Brain, bonuses, examples, guides, templates), with the personal sidebar excluded. New: one real agent run on a fictional candle store, rebuilt as a neutral chat window so no third-party logos appear. Bundle: real dashboard captures from 14 Oct (template grid, the template dialog before and after Copy JSON prompt, Start here). New: one chat run turning a product photo into a library ad.
MGOne HyperFrames template that rebuilds Reel B's grammar in each brand's own palette: show tag, section chip, two-line headline with an accent word, screenshot card with a highlight box, word-by-word captions, ghost word, count-up, checklist, step badges, stamp and end card. It's built once and reused by all four ads.
NarrationThe faceless ads use ElevenLabs in the professor register: full sentences, natural breaths, about 140 to 150 words a minute, delivery notes like [warm, measured, explaining]. Before the full run, I audition three voices on the first 20 s of EA-2 and send you the samples.
Music and soundOne continuous low bed per ad, ducked under the voice. The only added sounds are the count-up ticks and the approval stamp.
Organic finishNo logo until the script names the product. A show tag replaces the logo, and there's no permanent banner. Text stays clear of the Reels UI safe zones. Prices appear only on the end card, as a swappable unit.
